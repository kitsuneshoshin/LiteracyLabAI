Year 3 English (UK): what children learn and how to help

Year 3 children in the UK are usually aged 7–8 and are working on three main things in reading and writing, following England's National Curriculum and GCSE English. This guide explains each one in plain English, with something to try at home and practice prompts you can use today.

What Year 3 focuses on

Time, Place & Cause Conjunctions

Curriculum reference: Y3 statutory · English App. 2

Linking ideas with words that show when, where or why, such as 'before', 'while', 'because' and 'so'.

Try at home
Describe the school day using 'before', 'after' and 'because' in each sentence.

Organising Paragraphs Around a Theme

Curriculum reference: Years 3-4 statutory · Writing composition PoS

Grouping related sentences into paragraphs, each with its own main idea.

Try at home
Ask your child to write about a holiday in two paragraphs: one for the place and one for the activities.

Drawing Inferences With Evidence

Curriculum reference: Years 3-4 statutory · Reading comprehension PoS

Going beyond what is stated and backing the idea up with a specific detail from the text.

Try at home
Ask 'What makes you say that?' every time your child gives an opinion about a story.

How much should they write?

A good target for a primary years piece at this level is one or two short paragraphs, which is about 80 words when practising in LiteracyLab AI. Short and regular beats long and rare: one carefully improved piece teaches more than three rushed ones.

Three practice prompts to try this week

  1. Write the first paragraph of an adventure story that starts with a surprise.
  2. Explain how to make your favourite snack so a friend could follow it.
  3. Write a story about a day when everything went wrong, and how it ended.

Common questions

How much should a Year 3 child write?

One or two short paragraphs, or about 80 words is a sensible target for practice. Quality matters more than length.

What are the main skills at this level?

Time, Place & Cause Conjunctions; Organising Paragraphs Around a Theme; Drawing Inferences With Evidence. Each is explained above with something to try at home.

How do I know if my child is on track?

Compare their writing with the three focus areas above and ask their teacher for a view. A tool such as LiteracyLab AI can show, for each piece, what worked and the next step, but your child's teacher knows their progress best.

How to give feedback at home

  • Have your child plan in three bullet points first, then write.
  • Read the piece aloud together and let your child spot what to fix before you do.
  • Pick one thing to improve each time, such as an interesting opening, rather than correcting everything.
