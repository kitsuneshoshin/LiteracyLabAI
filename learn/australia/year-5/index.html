Year 5 English (Australia): what children learn and how to help

Year 5 children in Australia are usually aged 10–11 and are working on three main things in reading and writing, following the Australian Curriculum (version 9.0), set by ACARA. This guide explains each one in plain English, with something to try at home and practice prompts you can use today.

What Year 5 focuses on

Complex Sentences for Effect

Curriculum reference: AC9E5LA05 · Year 5 Language

Choosing a complex sentence on purpose to build suspense, show contrast or add detail.

Try at home
Rewrite a flat sentence as a complex one and say which version is more interesting and why.

Planning, Editing & Publishing Texts

Curriculum reference: AC9E5LY06 · Year 5 Literacy

Going through the full writing process: plan, draft, edit and present the final piece.

Try at home
Use a simple checklist: plan, write, check, tidy. Tick each stage off.

Comprehension Strategies

Curriculum reference: AC9E5LY05 · Year 5 Literacy

The habits good readers use to understand a text: predicting what comes next, re-reading when something does not make sense, and picturing what is happening.

Try at home
Pause halfway through a book and ask, 'What do you think happens next, and what in the story makes you think so?'

How much should they write?

A good target for a primary years piece at this level is one or two short paragraphs, which is about 210 words when practising in LiteracyLab AI. Short and regular beats long and rare: one carefully improved piece teaches more than three rushed ones.

Three practice prompts to try this week

  1. Write a story about a day when everything went wrong, and how it ended.
  2. Describe your favourite place without using the word 'nice'.
  3. Write a letter to a character from your favourite book.

Common questions

How much should a Year 5 child write?

One or two short paragraphs, or about 210 words is a sensible target for practice. Quality matters more than length.

What are the main skills at this level?

Complex Sentences for Effect; Planning, Editing & Publishing Texts; Comprehension Strategies. Each is explained above with something to try at home.

How do I know if my child is on track?

Compare their writing with the three focus areas above and ask their teacher for a view. A tool such as LiteracyLab AI can show, for each piece, what worked and the next step, but your child's teacher knows their progress best.

How to give feedback at home

  • Have your child plan in three bullet points first, then write.
  • Read the piece aloud together and let your child spot what to fix before you do.
  • Pick one thing to improve each time, such as an interesting opening, rather than correcting everything.
