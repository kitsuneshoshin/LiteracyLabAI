C1 Advanced English: what learners can do and how to practise

At CEFR C1 Advanced level, a learner is working towards three skills in reading and writing. This guide explains each one in plain English, with something to try at home and practice prompts you can use today.

What this level focuses on

Understanding Long & Complex Texts

Curriculum reference: CEFR C1 Reading

Following a long or dense text, tracking how its ideas connect from start to finish.

Try at home
Read a long article in sections and write one line after each section about what it added.

Expressing Points of View at Length

Curriculum reference: CEFR C1 Writing

Presenting and developing a point of view over several well-organised paragraphs.

Try at home
Write a 200-word piece on a topic you feel strongly about, with a clear start and end.

Writing About Complex Subjects Clearly

Curriculum reference: CEFR C1 Writing

Explaining difficult topics in clear, well-organised writing.

Try at home
Explain a hard idea from school to a younger relative in writing.

How much should they write?

A good target for a senior years piece at this level is a structured piece of several well-developed paragraphs, which is about 350 words when practising in LiteracyLab AI. Short and regular beats long and rare: one carefully improved piece teaches more than three rushed ones.

Three practice prompts to try this week

  1. Write the opening of a story in which the setting plays a central role.
  2. Analyse how a writer creates tension in a passage you have studied.
  3. Should the voting age be lowered? Argue your case.

Common questions

How much should a learner at this level write?

A structured piece of several well-developed paragraphs, or about 350 words is a sensible target for practice. Quality matters more than length.

What are the main skills at this level?

Understanding Long & Complex Texts; Expressing Points of View at Length; Writing About Complex Subjects Clearly. Each is explained above with something to try at home.

How do I know if my child is on track?

Compare their writing with the three focus areas above and ask their teacher for a view. A tool such as LiteracyLab AI can show, for each piece, what worked and the next step, but your child's teacher knows their progress best.

How to give feedback at home

  • Use timed practice: plan for five minutes, write, then spend five minutes proofreading.
  • Check each paragraph makes one clear point and links back to the question.
  • Ask your teen to say in one sentence what their piece is arguing before they begin.
