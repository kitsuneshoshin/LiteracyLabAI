Primary 6 English (Singapore & SE Asia): what's taught

Primary 6 children in Singapore and South-East Asia are usually aged 12–13 and are working on three main things in reading and writing, following Cambridge Primary, Lower Secondary and IGCSE / O-Level English. This guide explains each one in plain English, with something to try at home and practice prompts you can use today.

What Primary 6 focuses on

Reading Strand

Curriculum reference: Cambridge Primary English · Stage 6 Reading

The reading side of Cambridge English at this stage: understanding what is read, using clues in the text, and talking about it.

Try at home
Read together for ten minutes a day and talk about one thing that surprised you.

Writing Strand

Curriculum reference: Cambridge Primary English · Stage 6 Writing

The writing side of Cambridge English at this stage: writing for different purposes with clear sentences and organised ideas.

Try at home
Ask for a short piece each week on a topic your child chooses, and praise one thing that works.

Comprehension

Curriculum reference: Cambridge Primary English · Stage 6 Reading

Understanding a text well enough to answer questions about it, explain it and make sense of the ideas.

Try at home
After reading, ask three questions: one fact, one 'why', and one 'what do you think?'

How much should they write?

A good target for a primary years piece at this level is one or two short paragraphs, which is about 150 words when practising in LiteracyLab AI. Short and regular beats long and rare: one carefully improved piece teaches more than three rushed ones.

Three practice prompts to try this week

  1. Write the first paragraph of an adventure story that starts with a surprise.
  2. Explain how to make your favourite snack so a friend could follow it.
  3. Write a story about a day when everything went wrong, and how it ended.

Common questions

How much should a Primary 6 child write?

One or two short paragraphs, or about 150 words is a sensible target for practice. Quality matters more than length.

What are the main skills at this level?

Reading Strand; Writing Strand; Comprehension. Each is explained above with something to try at home.

How do I know if my child is on track?

Compare their writing with the three focus areas above and ask their teacher for a view. A tool such as LiteracyLab AI can show, for each piece, what worked and the next step, but your child's teacher knows their progress best.

How to give feedback at home

  • Have your child plan in three bullet points first, then write.
  • Read the piece aloud together and let your child spot what to fix before you do.
  • Pick one thing to improve each time, such as an interesting opening, rather than correcting everything.
