Story Mountain explained: plan a story step by step

The Story Mountain is a simple way to plan a story so it has a shape. Picture a mountain: the story climbs to an exciting peak and then comes back down. Children aged about 7 to 12 use it in many schools.

The five parts

  • Opening: introduce who and where.
  • Build-up: something starts to happen, and the tension grows.
  • Climax: the most exciting or tense moment, the top of the mountain.
  • Resolution: the problem gets sorted out.
  • Ending: how things settle, and how the character feels now.

A tiny example

  • Opening: Mia is camping with her dad by a lake.
  • Build-up: At night she hears a rustling outside the tent.
  • Climax: She unzips the tent and sees two glowing eyes.
  • Resolution: It is a hedgehog looking for crumbs.
  • Ending: Mia laughs, shares a biscuit and falls asleep.

How to use it

Draw a mountain on paper and write one line at each point before your child writes the story. Planning takes two minutes and fixes the most common problem in children's stories: a lot of build-up and then a rushed ending.

Common problems

  • No problem: the story is just a list of things that happened. Ask what goes wrong.
  • A climax that is not very exciting. Ask what the worst thing that could happen is.
  • An abrupt ending. Ask how the character feels at the end and what has changed.

A blank Story Mountain template to copy

Draw a mountain with five labelled points and give your child one box to fill at each point. You do not need a printed template: a folded sheet of paper works well.

  • Point 1, bottom left (Opening): Who is in the story? Where are they? When is it?
  • Point 2, up the slope (Build-up): What happens that starts the problem? What does the character want?
  • Point 3, the peak (Climax): What is the most exciting, scary or surprising moment?
  • Point 4, down the slope (Resolution): How is the problem solved, and who solves it?
  • Point 5, bottom right (Ending): How does the character feel now, and what has changed?

Turning the plan into a story

  • Give each point its own paragraph, or two for the build-up and climax.
  • Spend the most words at the peak. Slow down and add what the character sees, hears and feels.
  • Start each paragraph differently, so the story does not read as a list.
  • Read the story aloud when it is finished. Children often hear a missing word or a run-on sentence that they cannot see.

Story Mountain or other plans?

Some schools use a story spine, a five-finger plan or a simple beginning, middle and end instead. They all do the same job: give the story a shape before the writing starts. If your child's school uses a different plan, follow that one, and use the mountain as a back-up for stories that feel flat.

Make it their own

Once they can use the mountain, encourage them to change it: start in the middle of the action or end on a surprise. Structure is a starting point, not a cage.

Common questions

What age is the Story Mountain for?

It is most often used with children of about 7 to 12, but the idea works for any age.

Does every story need all five parts?

For planning, yes. Later they can vary or combine parts.

What are the parts of a Story Mountain?

Opening, build-up, climax, resolution and ending. The climax is the peak of the mountain.

How long should a Story Mountain plan take?

Two to five minutes. One short line at each point is enough.
