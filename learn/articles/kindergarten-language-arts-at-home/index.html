Kindergarten language arts: what it covers and how to help at home

Kindergarten language arts, often shortened to ELA, is the part of the school day where children learn to read, write, speak and listen. It is mostly play, songs, stories and drawing, and not worksheets. This guide explains what it usually covers and how to help at home. Schools and states vary, so check what your child's school expects.

Kindergarten language arts at a glance

  • Reading: learning letters and their sounds, hearing the sounds in words, and reading simple words and sentences.
  • Writing: drawing and writing about something they know, then building up to a short sentence.
  • Speaking and listening: taking turns, listening to a story and retelling it.
  • Language: learning new words, and starting to use capital letters and full stops.
  • How it feels: short, playful and full of stories. Many children do not read independently at first, and that is common.

Letters and sounds

Most kindergarten programs teach the letters of the alphabet and the sound each one makes, usually a few at a time. Children also learn to hear the separate sounds in a word: that "cat" has three sounds, c, a and t. This is the base for reading and spelling.

  • Sing alphabet songs and point to letters on signs, cereal boxes and book covers.
  • Play "I spy" with sounds: "I spy something that starts with mmm."
  • Clap the beats in names and words, then say the first sound of each one.
  • Make letters with playdough, chalk or a finger in sand, which links the shape to the sound.

Reading together

Reading aloud to your child every day is one of the most useful habits. Children who are read to hear new words, learn how stories work and associate books with comfort.

  • Let your child choose some books, and read favourites again and again.
  • Point to the words as you read, so they see that print goes left to right.
  • Pause before a rhyming word and let your child fill it in.
  • Ask simple questions: "What happened first?" and "What do you think will happen next?"
  • When they start reading, take turns: they read a page or a line, and you read the next.

Early writing

Writing starts as drawing and marks, and then letters and words. Many kindergarten programs ask children to draw a picture and tell or write something about it. Spelling is expected to be "sounded out", so a child who writes "I lik my dog" is doing exactly what is hoped.

  • Write their name and a few favourite words together.
  • Ask your child to draw a picture and tell you about it, then write down what they say, or help them write one word.
  • Praise the sounds they got right instead of correcting every spelling.
  • Make writing useful: a shopping list, a card for a grandparent or a label for a toy box.

Talking and listening

Conversation builds vocabulary and confidence. Ask open questions, give your child time to answer and add a new word to what they say: if they say "big dog", you might say "yes, a huge, fluffy dog". Retelling a story or a day in order, with a beginning, middle and end, is early practice for writing a narrative.

How to keep it calm

  • Keep sessions short, five to ten minutes, and stop while your child is still enjoying it.
  • Follow their interests: dinosaurs, trucks and princesses all make good reading and writing.
  • Do not compare with other children. Children learn to read at different speeds in these early years.
  • If you are worried about your child's progress or speech, talk to the teacher or your child's doctor.

Common questions

What is kindergarten language arts?

It is the part of kindergarten that teaches reading, writing, speaking and listening, including letters and sounds, sharing stories and early writing.

What does ELA mean in kindergarten?

ELA stands for English language arts. In kindergarten it covers the same things as language arts: reading, writing, speaking and listening.

Should my kindergartener be able to read?

Children develop at different speeds, and many are just beginning to read simple words at this age. Ask the teacher what your school expects and how your child is doing.

How can I help my kindergartener with writing?

Let them draw and tell you about their picture, praise the sounds they have written, and make writing useful with lists and cards. Do not worry about perfect spelling at this age.
