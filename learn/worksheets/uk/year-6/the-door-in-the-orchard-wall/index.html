The Door in the Orchard Wall

A mysterious story about a door that was not there yesterday, with eight questions on language, building mystery and predicting what happens next.

Year 6 (ages 10 to 11, UK)StoryAbout 30 minutes15 marks308 words

Read the passage

Everyone in the village of Aldermere knew that the old orchard was best avoided. Its wall was crumbling and its gate had rusted shut years ago. Even the village dogs crossed the lane to avoid it. Mia had always obeyed the rule, until the afternoon she saw the door.

It was set low in the stone, half hidden by ivy: a small, round door, painted the faded blue of a summer sky. Yesterday it had not been there. Mia was certain of that.

Her fingers trembled as she pushed. The door swung inward with a groan, and a sweet smell drifted out, of apples and warm honey and something older, like rain on dry earth. Beyond it, the orchard was not the tangled wilderness she had always seen over the wall. The trees stood in perfect rows, heavy with fruit that glowed faintly, like lanterns lit from within.

“Hello?” she called. Her voice sounded small. Nothing answered, but the branches rustled softly, as if the whole orchard had drawn a breath.

She stepped inside. Behind her, the door closed with a gentle click.

Mia spun round and tugged at the handle, but the wood had vanished into solid stone. Then, from somewhere among the glowing trees, she heard a sound that made her pause: a soft, steady humming, like someone who had been waiting a very long time and had decided to pass the hours with a song.

Mia swallowed. She pressed her palm flat against the stone. It was cold and rough and utterly ordinary, as if there had never been a door at all. Somewhere above her, a single apple dropped from a branch and rolled across the grass, stopping neatly at her feet. She could stay frozen beside the wall, or she could follow the music. Slowly, she squared her shoulders and began to walk.

Answer the questions

  1. What colour was the door? (1 mark)
  2. Find and copy one word from the first paragraph that tells you the orchard wall was falling apart. (1 mark)
  3. The writer says Mia “had always obeyed the rule, until the afternoon she saw the door”. What does this suggest about the door? (2 marks)
  4. “As if the whole orchard had drawn a breath.” What effect does this create? (2 marks)
  5. Find and copy the simile that describes the fruit. What does it suggest about the fruit? (2 marks)
  6. Give two ways the writer builds a feeling of mystery in this story. (2 marks)
  7. Summarise what happens in this extract in no more than three sentences. (3 marks)
  8. What do you think Mia will find if she follows the humming? Use clues from the story to explain your prediction. (2 marks)
Answer key for parents and teachers

1. Faded blue (the blue of a summer sky). (1 mark)

Finding information (2b). A detail to find in the second paragraph.

2. crumbling (1 mark)

Word meaning (2a). Finding the word that shows the state of the wall builds vocabulary and attention to detail.

3. The door was so surprising or tempting that it made Mia break a rule she had always followed (curiosity was stronger than obedience). (2 marks)

Inference (2d). A short phrase that tells us a lot about a character and a turning point.

4. It makes the orchard seem alive, as if it is watching or waiting, which makes the scene mysterious or slightly frightening. (2 marks)

Language choices. Explaining the effect of a writer’s choice, not just naming it.

5. “like lanterns lit from within”. It suggests the fruit gave off a warm, magical glow. (2 marks)

Language choices. Spotting a simile and then saying what it suggests.

6. Any two of: the door appears suddenly; the strange smell; the glowing fruit; the door vanishing; the unseen humming. (2 marks)

Explaining from the text. Seeing how a writer builds an atmosphere out of small details.

7. One mark each for: Mia finds a door that was not there before; she goes through into a magical, glowing orchard and the door vanishes behind her; she hears humming and decides to follow it. (3 marks)

Summarising (2c). A summary keeps the main events and leaves out the descriptions.

8. Any sensible prediction supported by a clue, for example: someone who has been waiting a long time, because the humming sounds like “someone who had been waiting a very long time”. (2 marks)

Giving a view with evidence. Predicting what happens next, and justifying it with a clue from the text.

What this practises

Finding information (2b), Word meaning (2a), Inference (2d), Language choices, Explaining from the text, Summarising (2c), Giving a view with evidence. The codes in brackets are the reading content domains from the national curriculum test framework. Marks and answers are a guide: accept any sensible answer that is backed up by the text.

Now try the writing task

Continue the story: what does Mia find at the end of the humming?

Aim for eight to twelve sentences. Read it back aloud together and fix anything that does not sound right.
